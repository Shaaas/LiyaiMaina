The practice is a direct response: to build work that is rigorous, rooted, and refuses to ask for permission.

Who We Are

A practice built
against the grain.

Q-Rator is a Nairobi-based multidisciplinary design practice founded by Liyai Maina. The work spans brand identity, editorial design, pattern systems, and cultural advocacy.

The name is deliberate. A curator selects what enters. Q-Rator questions who is doing the selecting — and builds tools to shift that balance.

Every project is approached as both a design problem and a cultural one. Who is this for? Who does it include? What does it make possible?

The Approach

Strategy first.
Aesthetics second.

The practice doesn't separate thinking from making. Strategy and visual form develop together — because the best brand systems are arguments, not just looks.

Work is grounded in research, built around clarity, and finished to a level that demands to be taken seriously.

What We Do

Services& capabilities.

01

Brand Identity

Full visual identity development from strategy through to final assets. This is the core of the practice — building systems that communicate clearly and endure. Every project includes a thorough discovery phase to understand the client's world before a mark is drawn.

Logo Design Brand Systems Typography Colour Strategy Brand Guidelines
02

Editorial & Print

Magazines, reports, menus, stationery, and publications. Editorial design is where content becomes form — where the layout itself makes an argument. The practice brings the same rigour applied to brand work to every printed surface.

Magazine Layout Stationery Menus Annual Reports Print Collateral
03

Cultural Strategy

Presentations, strategy decks, and communication frameworks for cultural organisations, artists, and advocacy groups. This work is about making arguments — about access, representation, and what creative industries owe their communities.

Strategy Decks Presentations Communication Design Advocacy Materials
04

Pattern & Textile Systems

Original pattern work grounded in African design languages. The Liyai Pattern series represents an ongoing investigation into visual rhythm, institutional form, and cultural belonging — available for licensing or bespoke commission.
